A forecast your CFO trusts isn't the most detailed one. It's the one built on drivers.
Step one: find the few drivers that move the business. Customers, price, churn, headcount.
Step two: separate assumptions from calculations. All assumptions on one sheet, clearly labeled, with the source next to each one.
Step three: forecast revenue from drivers, not from last year plus ten percent.
Step four: link the three statements, so profit, balance sheet and cash all move together.
Step five: build checks. The balance sheet balances, cash ties, and nothing is hard-coded inside a formula.
Step six: add scenarios. Base, downside, upside, switched with one cell.
Then compare the forecast to actuals every month and learn from the misses.
AI can help write formulas and spot errors. The assumptions stay yours.
