Averages lie. Cohorts don't.
A cohort analysis groups customers by the month they joined, and then follows each group over time.
Why does it matter? Imagine total revenue is growing. Looks great. But the cohort table shows that every new group of customers stays for a shorter time than the one before. Growth is hiding a retention problem.
In Excel it's three steps. One: for each customer, find the first purchase month. Two: for each transaction, calculate how many months passed since that first month. Three: build a pivot table, cohorts in the rows, months since joining in the columns, and show the percentage still active.
Add color scales, and the pattern jumps out.
AI can write the formulas for you, but you decide what counts as an active customer.
